\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Speaking to a small UAV is appealing because it matches how people communicate in shared physical spaces, but it is unsafe to treat open-ended speech as direct flight control. This paper frames talk-to-the-drone interaction as a safety-mediated systems problem. The central design choice is to convert short onboard audio windows into constrained intent-state events, where ordinary movement speech, emergency speech, and unsupported audio are separated before any control policy is allowed to act.

The resulting architecture gives the drone a local, auditable voice interaction layer under rotor self-noise. A noise-aware recognizer supplies the intent-state event, an embedded ESP32/TFLM path makes event generation possible near the vehicle, and a conservative bridge records whether the event is held, rejected, or forwarded. This boundary keeps the recognizer useful for human-drone interaction without pretending that classifier output alone is a safety mechanism.
