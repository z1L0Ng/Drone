\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents a safety-state mediated voice interaction architecture for talking to small UAVs under severe self-noise. The design converts natural speech into constrained onboard intent/state events and routes every event through an auditable control bridge. This framing is narrower than general ASR and more cautious than direct speech-to-flight control.

The recognizer combines rotor-noise-centered training with a publishable split between an anchor recognizer and an embedded student. The ESP32 prototype shows how the embedded student can become a deployment-constrained safety-event source for a command/state log. The evaluation and artifact plan keeps future extension concrete: baseline/deployment comparison, noise robustness, speaker and language robustness, and reproducible release manifests must be tied to result files before they become completed claims. The remaining system evidence is labeled acoustic validation, gate false-trigger evaluation, and no-propeller control-bridge logging so that the safety claim rests on bridge behavior rather than recognizer output alone.
