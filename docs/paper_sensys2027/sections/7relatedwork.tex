\section{Related Work}

\subsection{UAV Voice Interfaces Under Noise}

Speech control for UAVs has been explored through ASR-based command interfaces and multimodal extensions. Kite shows that UAV speech control is feasible in principle \cite{oneata19_interspeech}, and follow-on work studies multimodal recognition for more complex interaction settings \cite{oneata2021multimodal}. Other UAV-focused studies document how operating noise degrades voice-command discrimination \cite{miesikowska2021uavnoise,park2023uavvehicle}. Our design differs by centering emergency interruption under self-noise, rather than a broad command interface.

\subsection{End-to-End Spoken Intent Understanding}

Spoken language understanding has moved from transcript-first pipelines toward direct speech-to-intent models \cite{lugosch19_interspeech,kuo20_interspeech,mhiri20_interspeech,ijcai2021p538}. This supports our choice to predict intent classes from audio. The drone setting adds a different systems requirement: the intent model must be paired with embedded inference and an auditable bridge before it can serve as a safety component.

\subsection{Robust Transfer for Speech Models}

Transfer learning and teacher-student training are widely used to improve speech robustness under noisy conditions \cite{park2020improvednoisystudent,baevski2020wav2vec20,yang21c_interspeech}. We build on that direction through a clean-to-noisy training recipe, but keep the claim local: the anchor recognizer supports rotor-noisy drone emergency intent recognition, not universal speech robustness.

\subsection{Embedded Speech and Safety-Oriented Deployment}

Speech command and SLU resources such as SLURP and SLUE broaden the evaluation space for spoken understanding \cite{bastianelli-etal-2020-slurp,shon2022slue}, while multilingual benchmarks such as ML-SUPERB motivate future generalization questions \cite{shi23g_interspeech}. Those resources do not replace embedded systems measurement. The deployment contribution here is an embedded voice-event source paired with an auditable control interface.

This paper also differs from work that treats embedded speech as a standalone keyword or command-recognition task. The emergency voice component must report timing, confidence, and bridge state because its output may affect vehicle behavior. The embedded result is therefore not only a model artifact; it is a systems path from audio capture to a logged safety event.
