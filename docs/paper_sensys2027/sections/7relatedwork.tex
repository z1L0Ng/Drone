\section{Related Work}

\subsection{UAV Voice Interfaces Under Noise}

Speech control for UAVs has been explored through ASR-based command interfaces and multimodal extensions. Kite shows that UAV speech control is feasible in principle \cite{oneata19_interspeech}, and follow-on work studies multimodal recognition for more complex interaction settings \cite{oneata2021multimodal}. Other studies document how UAV operating noise degrades voice-command discrimination \cite{miesikowska2021uavnoise,park2023uavvehicle}. These papers establish the importance of the problem; our draft differs by centering an emergency-aware, onboard intent layer under rotor-like self-noise.

\subsection{End-to-End Spoken Intent Understanding}

Outside UAVs, spoken language understanding has moved from transcript-first pipelines toward direct speech-to-intent models \cite{lugosch19_interspeech,kuo20_interspeech,mhiri20_interspeech,ijcai2021p538}. This literature supports our choice to predict intent classes from audio rather than rely on ASR as an intermediate representation. The drone setting adds tighter constraints: the output vocabulary is intentionally small, self-noise is central, and an emergency miss has a different cost from an ordinary movement miss.

\subsection{Robust Transfer for Speech Models}

Transfer learning and teacher-student training are widely used to improve speech robustness under noisy conditions \cite{park2020improvednoisystudent,baevski2020wav2vec20,yang21c_interspeech}. We build on that direction, but the current paper should keep the claim narrower than general speech representation learning. The evidence supports a rotor-noisy drone intent-recognition pipeline anchored on \texttt{w14/preprocess\_ext}, not a universal speech robustness result.

\subsection{Speech Command and SLU Resources}

Spoken intent resources such as SLURP and SLUE broaden the evaluation space for speech understanding \cite{bastianelli-etal-2020-slurp,shon2022slue}, while multilingual benchmarks such as ML-SUPERB motivate broader generalization questions \cite{shi23g_interspeech}. These datasets and benchmarks are useful background for future extensions, including language and speaker variation. They do not replace closed-loop evaluation under UAV-relevant noise, embedded memory limits, and safety-oriented rejection behavior.
