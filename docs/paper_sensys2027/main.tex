\title{\sys: On-Device Emergency Intent Recognition Under Extreme Rotor Noise}

\begin{abstract}
Small drones need a lightweight way for nearby people to interrupt, redirect, or safely stop them in everyday environments. We argue that voice can serve as such a safety mechanism, but only if the system remains usable under the drone's own self-noise and without dependence on cloud infrastructure. This paper frames the problem as an on-device, emergency-aware audio-to-intent layer for drones operating near humans. The current design uses a narrow command abstraction, explicit rejection of uncertain audio, and noise-robust training based on class-aware augmentation and clean-to-noisy teacher-student transfer. Our present implementation is drone-centered and is intended to generalize across drone platforms rather than remain bound to a single Tello-style airframe. This draft therefore prioritizes story and system framing first, while using current noisy-benchmark and prototype evidence as the initial validation path. Future work may extend similar ideas to other nearby robots, but that is not the subject of this paper.
\end{abstract}
