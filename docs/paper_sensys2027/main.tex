\title{\sys: Voice-Driven UAV Safety Interaction Under Rotor Noise}

\begin{abstract}
Speaking to a nearby drone is a natural interaction mode, especially when a person cannot quickly reach a controller, phone application, or networked interface. However, rotor self-noise and vehicle-facing risk make common speech-interface designs insufficient: transcript-first parsing can be brittle on short noisy clips, keyword or command classifiers do not specify how speech should be mediated before action, and direct speech-to-command mapping can turn recognition errors into vehicle-facing action pressure. This paper introduces \sys{}, an onboard voice safety layer for small UAVs. The key idea is to map speech into safety-relevant intent states rather than treating it as a transcript, keyword trigger, or flight command. \sys{} uses a narrow intent-state mapping: movement represents ordinary control-oriented speech, emergency represents safety-critical speech, and unknown contains unsupported or uncertain audio. A safety-state boundary then decides how each intent-state update may influence the vehicle. We implement \sys{} with a noise-aware recognizer and an ESP32 runtime path that performs local audio capture, feature extraction, integer inference, and intent-state reporting, and we evaluate it against transcript-first, compact-classifier, and safety-state boundary ablation alternatives.
\end{abstract}
