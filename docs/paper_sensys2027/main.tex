\title{\sys: Safety-State Mediated Voice Interaction for Small UAVs Under Rotor Noise}

\begin{abstract}
Speaking to a nearby drone is a natural and lightweight interaction mode, but rotor self-noise and flight-control risk make direct speech-to-flight mapping unsafe. This paper presents a safety-state mediated voice interaction architecture for small UAVs. The system converts short onboard audio windows into constrained intent/state events under rotor self-noise and passes those events to a conservative control boundary rather than treating them as flight commands. The recognizer is deliberately narrow: emergency denotes safety-critical handling, movement denotes pending ordinary interaction that still needs downstream policy, and unknown/fallback keeps unsupported audio out of the control stack. We instantiate this architecture with a rotor-noise-centered anchor recognizer and an ESP32/TFLM deployment candidate that emits local safety events through microphone capture, log-mel extraction, int8 inference, and USB CDC reporting on a XIAO ESP32-S3. Existing evidence supports the noisy-set recognizer anchor and the embedded runtime event path. Labeled real-world acoustic behavior, bridge validation, and live-flight safety claims remain outside the completed evidence for this draft.
