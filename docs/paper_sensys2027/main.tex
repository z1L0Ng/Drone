\title{\sys: On-Device Intent Recognition for Voice-Driven UAV Safety Interaction Under Rotor Noise}

\begin{abstract}
Speaking to a nearby drone is a natural and lightweight interaction mode, but rotor self-noise and flight-control risk make naive speech-to-command execution unsafe. This paper presents a drone-side voice-driven UAV safety interaction layer that turns short onboard audio windows into auditable intent/state events for a conservative control bridge. The recognizer is deliberately narrow: movement captures ordinary control-oriented speech, emergency captures safety-critical speech, and unknown keeps unsupported audio out of the control stack. In this design, intent recognition is the safety mechanism that makes talk-to-the-drone interaction more accountable: it constrains the command vocabulary, maps voice intent into safety-relevant states, and logs confidence before any action policy runs. We implement the recognizer with a noise-aware anchor model and an embedded student for ESP32/TFLM deployment. The prototype runs microphone capture, log-mel extraction, int8 inference, and USB CDC event reporting on a XIAO ESP32-S3. Offline noisy-set results establish the recognizer anchor, while board-side runtime measurements establish the embedded event path. The evaluation plan covers baseline comparison, rotor-noise robustness, deployment feasibility, speaker and language robustness protocols, and reproducible artifact packaging, with system-level validation required before any live-flight safety claim.
