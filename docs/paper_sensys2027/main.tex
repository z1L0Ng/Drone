\title{\sys: On-Device Emergency Voice Interruption for Small UAVs Under Rotor Noise}

\begin{abstract}
Small drones operating near people need an emergency interruption path that does not depend on a handset, cloud service, or perfect network connection. This paper presents a drone-side voice safety component for that setting. The component listens through rotor self-noise, recognizes short emergency-oriented intent events, and exposes those events to a conservative control bridge rather than treating speech as a direct flight-command channel. The recognizer is deliberately narrow: emergency interruption is the primary event, movement is an auxiliary intent class, and unknown is a guardrail for audio that should not enter the control stack. We implement the recognizer with a noise-aware anchor model and an embedded student for ESP32/TFLM deployment. The prototype runs microphone capture, log-mel extraction, int8 inference, and USB CDC event reporting on a XIAO ESP32-S3. Offline noisy-set results establish the recognizer anchor, while board-side runtime measurements establish the embedded event path. The remaining validation is system-level: labeled real-world acoustic testing, false-trigger accounting, and no-propeller control-bridge logs are required before making live-flight safety claims.
